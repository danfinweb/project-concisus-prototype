# Concisus – Story & Lore

Nov 22, 2026 · @Dan

## Setting and tone

Veligrad, a former Soviet republic that refused to let the Soviet Union go, has been a permanent proxy battlefield for three decades. The game starts in the present day (early 2020s), as independent PMCs — the player's among them — enter a war that nobody is allowed to win.

This doc holds the story and lore of the game. How factions, events and systems work in play is defined in the [Game Design Document](https://claude.ai/artifact/EntHEaJm8q32rPTHWXMbvo).

**Tone:** grounded and serious. No jokes at the war's expense; the strangeness comes from the people who run it. The great powers are never named — only the eastern and the western influence.

## History of Veligrad

The war began with Orlov's coup in 1992 and has never been allowed to end.

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Today | Veligrad is no longer a state but a permanent battlefield. Peace is politically inconvenient, victory deliberately out of reach. Independent PMCs arrive whose choices could unite, reform or doom the country. |
| Later | The trade networks between the front lines unite under one written code as the Krug. |
| Early 2000s | Two rival PMCs dominate the battlefield: Varyag Solutions for the SSV, Aegis Group for the VDK. Their officially denied clashes become the war's most decisive battles. |
| Mid-1990s onward | Between the front lines the state stops existing: no wages, no police, no courts. Villages, deserters and smugglers start setting their own rules to survive. |
| By 1994 | Open civil war. Volkov later returns to lead the Veligrad Democratic Coalition (VDK). The eastern influence arms and advises the SSV as "fraternal aid"; the western influence covertly funds, trains and supplies the VDK. Both sides turn to PMCs for deniability. |
| 1992 | Marshal Mikhail Orlov, Chief of the General Staff, leads a coup. Volkov's government is dissolved; the Socialist State of Veligrad (SSV) declares itself the rightful successor to Soviet authority. The army splits along ideological and regional lines and armed resistance starts almost at once. Volkov survives the purges and goes into exile. When the army splits, Veligrad's leftover Soviet tactical warheads are divided too; both the SSV and the VDK are believed to hold a few. |
| 1991 | The Soviet Union dissolves. Veligrad's military and political elites call the collapse illegal — a temporary betrayal of socialism, not its end. Interim President Aleksandr Volkov pushes for democratic reform. Closed Soviet research and production cities in the woods and mountains are sealed off and vanish from every map. |

## Factions and leaders

Six armed powers fight over Veligrad, with two unnamed great powers behind them.

| Faction | What it is | Leader | Backed by | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Socialist State of Veligrad (SSV) | Military-socialist regime, the formal government | Marshal Mikhail Orlov | Eastern influence: weapons, intelligence, advisers | Soviet continuity, central control |
| Veligrad Democratic Coalition (VDK) | Opposition of ex-officers and civilian reformers | Aleksandr Volkov | Western influence, covertly: money, training, logistics | Elections, decentralisation, Western integration |
| Varyag Solutions | Eastern-aligned PMC, officially "advisers" to the SSV; Soviet doctrine with modern tactics | Anton Sokolov, former eastern special forces commander | SSV and the eastern influence | Contracts and keeping the SSV standing |
| Aegis Group | Western PMC, officially protection and training for the VDK; better-equipped and high-tech | Daniel Rook, former western officer | VDK and the western influence | Contracts and deniable combat against the SSV |
| The Krug | Self-governed network of traders, smugglers, deserters and village defence groups | Viktor Karsin, keeper of the Code | Itself: tithes from routes and markets | To survive and earn a living on its own terms, whoever holds the capital |
| Player's PMC | Independent private military company | The player | Whoever pays | Profit — or whatever the player decides |

The **eastern influence** and the **western influence** never appear openly and are never named. They shape the war from off the map through deliveries, intelligence and escalation.

**Leaders' habits**

Each leader carries one habit shaped by thirty years of war. It stays in the background but colours how they deal with the player.

| Leader | Faction | Personal habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marshal Mikhail Orlov | SSV | Speaks of "the Union" as if it never ended and insists on formal protocol. Signs every order by hand. |
| Aleksandr Volkov | VDK | Has not slept in the same building twice since the purges. Distrusts anyone who is early. |
| Anton Sokolov | Varyag Solutions | Courteous to a fault. Writes a short letter to the families of enemy contractors his men kill. |
| Daniel Rook | Aegis Group | Reviews footage of every engagement and keeps detailed files on his opponents. |
| Viktor Karsin | The Krug | Keeps all accounts by hand, in pencil, and erases debts once they are paid. Rarely stays in one place. |

## The Krug

*Krug* means "circle". Inside the circle the same rules bind everyone; outside it, you are a customer or a threat. In criminal slang the word also means a closed inner circle — a reading the members never deny.

**Origin.** After the coup, the land between the front lines lost everything a state provides. Salaries stopped, police and courts disappeared, and whichever army passed through took what it wanted. People survived by trading: fuel for grain, ammunition for medicine, safe passage for a share. Deserters guarded convoys, villages ran markets, smugglers knew the roads.

Viktor Karsin, a former army quartermaster who had kept fuel and grain moving across the lines since the 1990s, did not take command — he wrote things down. His Code turned scattered deals into one network. The Krug is criminal by any government's definition, but it is the only institution in Veligrad that keeps its word.

**The Code.** Hard, public and the same for members, outsiders and the player. Some members resent all foreign soldiers, the player's included.

## World lore

**Coups.** Veligrad began with a coup, and it can end with one.

**Hidden cities.** Deep in the woods and mountains lie closed cities from the Soviet era: research and production sites that were never on public maps and were sealed off when the Union fell.

**The warheads.** When the army split in 1992, Veligrad's leftover Soviet tactical warheads were divided too. Both the SSV and the VDK are believed to hold a few.

**Flavour events**

These have no gameplay effect. They appear only in the news feed, on radio intercepts or as visual changes on the map, and make Veligrad feel lived in.

| Event | When | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coup anniversary broadcast | Every year on the date of Orlov's coup | SSV state radio plays the Soviet anthem at midnight, followed by a speech "in the name of the Union" |
| Night of candles | Anniversary of the 1992 purges | Candles in windows across VDK-held towns; a list of names read on coalition radio |
| Village wedding | Random, in a town at peace | Celebratory shots in the air reported in the region; music on local radio |
| First snow | Start of winter | The map turns white; roads show tyre tracks; the news feed notes the first frost |
| Burning fields | End of harvest season | Smoke columns over the steppe; farmers burning stubble |
| Krug fair day | A few times a year in a large Krug market | Market lit with lanterns; traders from all sides in one place; the intel log mentions an unusually crowded road |
| Football across the line | Random, during a local ceasefire | Rumour that SSV and VDK conscripts played a match between the trenches; both sides deny it the next day |
| Foreign journalist | Random, in a large city | Reports of a foreign reporter asking about PMCs; she leaves the country a few days later |
| Blackout in the capital | Random | The capital goes dark on the map for one night; the government blames sabotage, the opposition blames incompetence |
| Graffiti | Random, in contested towns | New slogans on walls, seen in tactical fights there: "Who pays, rules", "The Union never ended", "Go home, soldiers" |
| Orthodox Easter | Every spring | Church bells on local radio; services held behind the lines on both sides |
